SYNOPSIS
--------
A boy in a small town turns ten and oversleeps on the morning he is due to
receive his first Pokémon. The three starters are gone. The professor has one
creature left and warns there is a problem with it. The boy takes it anyway. It
is an electric mouse that does not want him. He is given a handbook and six
empty balls and sent out. He fails to catch anything, throws a stone at the
wrong bird, and starts a flock. A girl pulls him out of a river. He loses her
bicycle. He puts his own body over the mouse and the mouse saves them both. A
large bird crosses a rainbow at the end and nobody can tell him what it was.


BEATS — numbered, in order
--------------------------
1.  Morning. The boy wakes late, in his sleep clothes, and runs.
2.  The laboratory. All three starter balls are empty. He begs.
3.  The professor has one left. He says there is a problem with it. The boy does
    not care.
4.  The mouse comes out and shocks him on contact. It will not come near him.
5.  The handbook and six balls are handed over. [ON DEMAND: the professor's
    parting line.]
6.  Out on the road. The mouse follows at a distance and will not be carried.
7.  A small brown bird. The boy throws a ball at it and nothing happens.
8.  The handbook, consulted: a wild creature has to be weakened before it can be
    taken. This is the first time the object is used on page.
9.  He tries to fight the bird with the mouse. The mouse refuses. He loses.
10. He throws a stone at what he believes is the same bird. It is a different
    bird, and a larger one, and it is angry.
11. The flock comes. He carries the mouse and goes over a fall into a river.
12. A red-haired girl fishes them out and tells him to get the mouse to a
    recovery centre in the next town.
13. He takes her bicycle without asking. The flock catches them on the road.
14. He puts his own body over the mouse. This is the episode's centre.
15. The mouse sees it. The mouse discharges, scatters the flock, and destroys the
    bicycle in the process.
16. He carries the mouse the rest of the way. The mouse licks his face.
17. The clouds open. A large bird crosses a rainbow. The handbook has no entry
    for it. [ON DEMAND: what the boy says when he sees it.]

KEY WORLD-STATE FACTS
---------------------
- The handbook is an ordinary carried object. It is consulted, it is wrong about
  things it has never seen, and it is not a companion. This matters enormously
  to this serial: it is the instrument that cannot name the OC.
- Recovery centres exist in each town and injured creatures are taken to them.
- Trainers carry a fixed number of empty balls when they set out.
- The rainbow bird is not in the handbook. Canon establishes, in episode one,
  that the handbook has gaps. That is a receipt, and it is the receipt this
  serial most depends on.


WARP POINTS — suggestions for the divergence ledger
---------------------------------------------------
W1  The OC joins the boy in this episode (R16). Canon gives him one creature.
    Ours gives him two. Everything downstream that counts his team is now off by
    one, and every such moment needs a receipt.
W2  Beat 8 — the handbook is consulted and answers. If the OC is present, the
    handbook may be pointed at it and return nothing, in the same episode where
    it correctly identifies a bird. The contrast is free and it is on-canon.
W3  Beat 14 — the body over the mouse. If the OC witnesses this, it is watching
    a human take a blow for a creature that has done nothing for him. For an
    animal that has been alone for years, that is the single most consequential
    thing it could see.
W4  Beat 17 — the rainbow bird, absent from the handbook. The episode already
    ends on "the handbook does not know everything." The OC is the same shape of
    unknown. Do not spend both in the same breath.
W5  The rival begins the same day with a different starter. Adding a second
    creature to the boy's first day changes what the rivalry is measuring.
    Logged, not resolved here.
