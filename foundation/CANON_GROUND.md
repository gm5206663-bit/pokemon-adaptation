## 1. The entry point — IL001

**Claim:** Season 1, Episode 1 is *"Pokémon, I Choose You!"*, Indigo League, set
in Kanto.
**Confidence:** `canon`
**Sources:** Wikipedia ("Pokémon, I Choose You!" — first episode of the Pokémon
anime, first broadcast 1997-04-01 on TV Tokyo, directed Masamitsu Hidaka, written
Takeshi Shudo); pallettown.fandom.com/wiki/IL001 (Japan 1997-04-01, US
1998-09-08).

**On-page beats, all available to the serial:**
- Ash Ketchum, ten years old, Pallet Town, oversleeps on the day he is due to
  receive his first Pokémon.
- All three starters are already taken. Professor Oak has one left, with "a
  problem". It is **Pikachu**.
- Pikachu shocks him on pickup, is aloof, openly distrusts him.
- Oak gives Ash a **Pokédex and six Poké Balls**.
- Ash tries to catch a Pidgey, fails, learns from the Pokédex that a Pokémon
  must be weakened first.
- Ash throws a rock at what he believes is the Pidgey. It is a **Spearow**. A
  flock comes.
- Ash and Pikachu dive a waterfall; **Misty** fishes them out and directs them
  to the Pokémon Center in **Viridian City**.
- Ash "borrows" Misty's bike. The flock catches them. **Ash shields Pikachu with
  his own body.** Pikachu, seeing that, drives the flock off with Thundershock
  and destroys the bike.
- Ash carries Pikachu into Viridian. Pikachu licks his cheek.
- **Major events on page:** Ash starts his journey with Pikachu · Gary starts his
  journey with Squirtle · Ash meets Misty · **Ash sees a Ho-Oh.**

**Why this matters to a Bagon POV:** the episode's emotional centre is *a
creature that does not trust the human, watching the human take a blow for it*.
Our OC is present in the same world on the same day and has no human at all.
That contrast is the opening, and it costs canon nothing.

**Firewall note:** the **Pokédex is an object in this episode**, and it is used
on page. Per R13, it has no data on a Bagon. That is a receipt-backed beat, not
an invention.

## 3. Bagon — the lore that becomes the power law

**Claim:** Bagon dreams of flying and, failing, hurls itself off cliffs.
**Confidence:** `canon`
**Sources:** Ruby — *"Bagon has a dream of one day soaring in the sky. In doomed
efforts to fly, this Pokémon hurls itself off cliffs. As a result of its dives,
its head has grown tough and as hard as tempered steel."* Sapphire — *"harbors a
never-ending dream of one day soaring high among the clouds... slams its hard
head against huge rocks and shatters them into pebbles."*

**Claim — and this is the important one:** *"Some theories suggest that its
behavior of forcefully bashing its head into things **stimulates cells that
affect its evolution**."*
**Confidence:** `canon`
**Source:** Pokémon Ultra Moon Pokédex entry, via Bulbapedia and
nintendo.fandom.com.

**Why this is load-bearing:** canon has already ruled that the headbutting is a
**cause** of evolution, not a symptom. That is a receipt for a mechanism, which
is what your method requires before a power law is written. It means the Bagon's
cliff-diving is not character colour — it is *training*, and the serial can
build on it without inventing a mechanism. This is the single best receipt in
the file. (It is not a licence for a meter system — that was struck; see
`_archive/2026-09-30_system_framework_struck/RECEIPT.md`.)

**Claim:** Salamence's Flying typing is where the dream resolves. Shelgon is a
pupa-like stage — the second such stage in an otherwise reptilian line, the
other being Pupitar.
**Confidence:** `canon`
**Sources:** pokemon.fandom.com/wiki/Shelgon; Bulbapedia type data.

**Claim:** Salamence's base stat total is 500, which is why it is counted among
the pseudo-legendaries.
**Confidence:** `reported` — sourced from pokemon-archive.fandom.com only, a
weaker source. **Do not put a number in prose anyway** (clean-and-clear law: no
count-numbers in the body). Held as background only.

## 4. Adaptation Talent — inherited, not invented

**Source of record:** `soul-land-universal-kit/blue_silver/ADAPTATION_TALENT.md`
**v2.0, locked universal core**, 157 KB. Also mirrored at
`SL_ARCHIVE/inbox/Adaptation-Talent-Definitive-Master-Foundation.md` and
`Soul_Land_3_Project/FRAMEWORK/CODEX/reference/`.

**Confidence:** `user ruling` — it is the author's own law, locked.

**Definition (verbatim from the Qian Xun Ji module's reference to the core):**
*"Innate pure inseparable non-sentient existential principle present from
conception that continuously uses holder's real existence experience information
resources intentions recovery relationships accessible real."*

**What it is NOT (verbatim):** *"Not AI not system window menu status screen
game mechanic not voice narrator guide adviser companion spirit familiar second
mind not separate consciousness not parasite symbiote possession blessing
detachable."*

**Three Domains:** Direct (the holder's own continuous existence) · Interface
(bonded weapons, artifacts, symbiotes, spirits, bonds, technology,
organizations, territory) · Authored Derivative (bounded adaptive forces).

**The Five Questions for every stage crossing (Master §5):** *What changed? What
caused it? What did he already possess that made this possible? What failed
first? What limitation remains?*

**Why this fits a Bagon unusually well:** the "not a system window, not a status
screen, embodied only" clause is the exact defence Pokémon needs. The franchise
is shaped like a game and pulls every writer toward menus, levels and
type-charts. The locked core forbids that at the definition level, so the law is
already written and does not have to be argued later.
