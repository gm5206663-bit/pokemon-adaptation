### R13 — Bagon is not native to Kanto in 1997. How did he get there?

**The receipts** (full detail in `CANON_GROUND.md`):

- Bagon is **#0371, Dragon-type, introduced in Generation III**. Bulbapedia
  states plainly: *"This Pokémon was unavailable prior to Generation III."*
- Its game debut is Ruby and Sapphire, **2002** — five years after IL001 aired
  on **1997-04-01**.
- Its only wild location in its debut generation is **Meteor Falls, Hoenn**. In
  the Kanto remakes FireRed and LeafGreen it is **trade only**.
- Its anime debut is **"Let Bagons be Bagons", AG043** — Advanced Generation,
  the Hoenn era. It does not appear in the Indigo League at all.

So a Bagon standing in Kanto on the day Ash receives Pikachu is a **logged
divergence**, and it needs a cause. This is not a flaw in the premise — it is
the premise's best hook. In Season 1 the Pokédex is a major object, and nobody
in Kanto has data on this creature. Ash's Pokédex returning nothing is a
stronger opening image than any speech.

**Options:**

| | Cause | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Egg or trade, moved west years ago** — someone brought it out of an unnamed eastern land and it was always here, unrecorded | Cleanest. Requires no canon event. The world simply has a gap in its map, which it does |
| **B** | **Shipped as cargo and lost** — a crate off a Hoenn-bound vessel, the ship's manifest wrong, the animal loose | Explains why nobody is looking for it, and why it is alone |
| **C** | **Held in a lab or a private collection and escaped** | Gives an antagonist thread from chapter 1 |
| **D** | **The author's own cause** | — |

**Recommendation: A**, with the eastern land never named in Season 1. Hoenn does
not exist as a place anyone in Kanto can point to yet, and naming it would
spend a reveal the anime does not spend for another five years.

### R14 — Bagon's line has a terminal. F14 says nothing has one.

Canon's line is **Bagon → Shelgon (level 30) → Salamence (level 50)**, and
Salamence is Dragon/Flying — the creature that dreamed of flying finally flies.
That is a **terminal**, and your Evolution Chain Law v5.1 says in terms:
*"There is no such thing as 100% MASTERED... no terminal — 100% is
evolution/fusion trigger."*

This is the most interesting conflict in the serial and it is yours to rule:

| | Ruling | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Canon's line is the visible chain, and Adaptation Talent continues it past Salamence** | Salamence becomes a floor, not a ceiling. Consistent with F14 and with how you ran Adaptation Talent in Qian Xun Ji. Costs the clean emotional payoff of "he can fly at last" |
| **B** | **The species line terminates, but the *techniques* do not** | Salamence is the last body; every move, sense and skill keeps evolving Low → Mid → High forever. Keeps canon's payoff AND keeps F14 |
| **C** | **Canon wins; F14 is suspended for species evolution in this serial** | Honest and simple, but it retires your strongest law for the whole run |

**Recommendation: B.** The body has an ending; the craft does not. And canon
hands you the mechanism for free — Pokémon Ultra Moon's own Pokédex entry says
*"Some theories suggest that its behavior of forcefully bashing its head into
things stimulates cells that affect its evolution."* Canon has already ruled
that the headbutting is a *cause* of evolution, which is exactly the kind of
receipt your method demands before a power law is written down.
