## This holder

**One truth, not two.** Unlike Qian Xun Ji, there is no second memory set. The
Bagon was **born here**. It has no Earth life, no past, no meta. R4 ruled
*rules, not events*: the OC understands types and evolution as a system and has
never heard of the plot.

**Where "rules not events" comes from, honestly.** It is not a downloaded
database and it must never be written as one. It is what any creature raised
inside a world learns by living in it — that fire beats grass, that water beats
fire, that a body changes shape at a threshold. The Bagon knows the *shape* of
the system the way a fish knows water. It does not know the *schedule*. It has
no idea who Ash Ketchum is, what Team Rocket wants, or that a Ho-Oh will cross a
rainbow this afternoon.

**This distinction is load-bearing.** If a scene ever has the OC anticipating a
canon event, the scene is wrong and the firewall in `RELATIONSHIPS.md` was
breached.

The Qian Xun Ji module names its tier *Mortal Divine Level* because that holder
is a 95-level Title Douluo. **This holder is at the opposite end of the scale**
and the module must say so plainly, or the next agent will write it strong
because the talent is famous.

| | Qian Xun Ji | This Bagon |
|---|---|---|
| Starting power | 95, Title Douluo | a newborn first-stage Pokémon |
| Tier | Mortal Divine Level | **Ordinary. Unremarkable. The floor** |
| Why the talent is strong | the holder is already immense | **it is not strong yet, and that is the point** |

**The talent is not a multiplier on power. It is a multiplier on the *rate at
which real experience converts into capability*.** A holder with nothing converts
nothing. This Bagon starts with a hard head, a bad temper about cliffs, and no
trainer. Everything it becomes, it becomes slowly and on the page.

## What the talent does NOW, at the floor

- **Converts repetition into progress.** The hundredth headbutt teaches the neck
  something the first did not. This is the Ultra Moon receipt doing real work:
  *"its behavior of forcefully bashing its head into things stimulates cells that
  affect its evolution."* Canon has already ruled the mechanism.
- **Converts injury into structure.** Every blow taken without yielding leaves
  the body differently built afterwards. Recovery is one of the core's named
  inputs, and it is not counted — it is lived.
- **Reads its own body accurately.** It knows when something is about to give,
  and when a threshold is near. Embodied, not narrated — a tightness, a heat, a
  wrongness in the shoulder.
- **Integrates what it touches.** Domain **Interface** is nearly empty at the
  start: it has no bonded object, no human, no territory. That emptiness is a
  story engine, not a gap. The first bond it forms will be the first time
  Interface has anything to work on.

## What the talent does NOT do

- It does not grant rungs. It prices them.
- It does not create from nothing. Every advance needs a cause.
- It does not speak, show, display, or explain. **Embodied only.**
- It does not bypass a wall. A locked flight line stays locked until the body
  earns it.
- It does not let the OC know the plot. R4 forbids it and the firewall enforces
  it.
- It does not stack. There is one talent, one holder, one continuous existence.

## The five questions — applied to the first crossing

Worked example, so the pattern is not abstract. **A winter of hitting the same
boulder.**

1. **What changed?** The neck stopped bruising. The impact that used to leave it
   dizzy now leaves it hungry.
2. **What caused it?** A winter of the same rock. Not a montage, not a feeling,
   and not a number — a season, shown by what the body can now carry.
3. **What did he already possess?** A skull canon describes as hard enough to
   cleave a boulder, and Rock Head, which removes the recoil tax that would
   otherwise cap the repetition.
4. **What failed first?** The aim. The first two hundred impacts landed on the
   crown, not the brow, and taught the wrong muscles.
5. **What limitation remains?** It can break stone. It cannot break a Pokémon
   that moves, because the target was never the problem — the timing was.

**That last line is the whole method.** A crossing that leaves no limitation is
a crossing that was not written honestly, and it is the reason power inflation
kills serials.

## Test every scene

Before a scene is written, ask:

- Did the OC's body actually do the thing that earns the advance?
- Is there a cause, or did the plot need it?
- Is the talent being *shown* through the body, or *explained* to the reader?
  Explanation is forbidden.
- Is the OC knowing something it has no way to know?
- What failed? If nothing failed, the scene is too easy.
- What is still out of reach afterwards?

If any answer is wrong, the scene is rewritten. The talent is the serial's most
likely source of rot and it gets checked harder than anything else.

## Locks

- The talent is **never named on page**. It has no name in the world. Nobody in
  Kanto has a word for it, because nobody in Kanto has seen it.
- The talent is **never displayed**. No light, no chime, no sensation described
  as information.
- The talent **never replaces a canon cause** (k16, and the MCU law: deny ACCESS,
  not competence).
- The talent **never lets the OC skip a rung**.
