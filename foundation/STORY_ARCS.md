# STORY_ARCS.md — the arc plan

**Shape only.** No arc is detailed beyond what the body's own stages already
force. Detailing an arc before its canon ore exists is how a plan gets built on
a canon fact that turns out to be wrong.

Rebuilt 2026-09-30 after the System framework was struck. The old version had
five arcs organised around meters reaching percentages. Those arcs are gone.

---

## Arc 1 — Stone

**Subject:** a creature alone in a region that does not know its name.

**What it covers:** a winter, maybe longer. The same boulder. The same ledge.
Canon says this species dreams of flying and, failing, hurls itself off cliffs —
Ruby's word for the attempts is **doomed**, and this arc is about being doomed
at something and doing it anyway.

**What it ends on:** not a threshold. A difference the body can feel and the
reader can see — the rock losing, for the first time, to something that in
autumn would have lost.

**Canon contact:** minimal by design. This is the arc where the OC is nowhere
near Ash. If it touches IL001 at all, it touches the same sky.

**The risk in this arc, named:** repetition. A serial about a creature hitting a
rock can become a serial about nothing. The defence is the five questions —
especially *"what failed first?"* — which force every stretch of time to teach
something different from the last.

## Arc 2 — The First Face

**Subject:** being seen.

**What it covers:** the first human encounter. Which human is **author-gated and
not proposed here**, because it is the serial's most consequential decision after
the premise. `RELATIONSHIPS.md` holds the candidates and their
`earliest_change` fields.

**What it must survive:** the Pokédex has no entry. Whoever holds it gets
silence, and the serial has to decide what a person does with a creature their
tool cannot name.

**What it ends on:** the Interface domain of the locked core getting its first
input. Nothing about the talent has had anything to work on until now.

## Arc 3 — Shelgon

**Subject:** waiting.

**What it covers:** the body stops moving. Canon's Shelgon is a cocoon. An
entire arc with nothing to do but be inside it.

**Why this is the bravest arc in the plan:** it is the one most likely to be cut
for pacing, and cutting it would be the mistake. Stillness is the second half of
what this species is, and a serial that skips it has only written the half about
hitting things.

## Arc 4 — Salamence

**Subject:** the dream, deferred for two whole stages, arriving.

**What it covers:** wings. Canon's own resolution — Salamence is Dragon /
Flying — and this serial needs to add nothing to make it land.

**The cost, recorded so it is never quietly forgotten:** R14-A ruled the chain
continues. So flying is not the end of the story. The moment still lands; it just
does not close anything, and the author chose that knowingly.

## Arc 5+ — After

**Subject:** unruled, and staying unruled.

> Author, on the past-Salamence stages: **"We later see this thing."**

Nothing past the wings is planned. No names are held in reserve. Planning it now
would be inventing a future the story has not earned.

---

## The rule underneath all of it

**Every arc boundary is a change in the body or in who knows what — never a
chapter count.** An arc ends when something real has happened to the creature,
or when a firewall changes state. Arcs never end because enough pages have
passed.
